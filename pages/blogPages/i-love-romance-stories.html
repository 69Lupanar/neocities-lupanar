Why I love romance


I originally prepared multiple sections studying the presence of romances in books, movies and games, the demographics of each medium, the types of stories they tell, and I had a blog page ready with multiple sections each diving into detail into how romance works and how the genre is presented and exploited by professional and hobbyist authors alike.

But then I came to a revelation: I have no idea what the fuck I'm talking about xD. My personal experience with romance is sadly quite limited and can be summed up with a couple fanfictions from my adolescence from Wattpad and AO3, and some of the few visual novels I played years ago. I am in no place to judge people's tastes or to pull out an arbitrary conclusion on the state of romance in modern media and the adult scene.

Instead, I'll just say that I fucking love romance. It's the main type of content I consume whenever I have the opportunity to. I love everything about it; the first contact, the slow build-up of tension, characters spending more and more time with each other and progressively learning more about themselves, the feelings, the confession...

To me, it's a very personal experience. It's not simply about the escapism, but also to meet and observe unique characters go about their daily lives and learn more about themselves as romance comes and turns their life upside down. It's about how they overcome those obstacles and become a better person along the way. Romance is a journey, and as someone who's never experienced that before, I find some of the stories I've read intimately fascinating, both as a craft and as a context.

Why don't we see more romance stories in general?


I mean, this isn't an issue exclusive to the adult scene. Regular books and movies also suffer from a lack of representation of love to a certain extent. In those not exclusively dedicated to a romance story, romance is often a secondary to tertiary plot point, one often used as a reward for the hero once he's saved the day. Oftentimes, the romance is poorly developed, the characters form an unhealty couple, and many problems in the story arise from a lack of communication. Romance is more often than not an afterthought, and clearly shoved in there to fill a checklist of cliches.

But video games have it the worst. When the romance isn't straight up terrible, it's inexistant. At first glance, it seems there isn't a huge appetite for that kind of genre outside of visual novels, and those are an extremely tiny niche. Granted, the interactivity aspect makes it significantly harder to integrate romance in an organic way, but it's not an impossible task, and it has been done before.

And that's doubly true for porn games. The days of the old flash gallery are long gone. Players now want their sexual interactions to feel earned, genuine. It's slow, yes, and not guaranteed to even work, but when it does, it's incredibly satisfying to see the fruits of your work. You earned this relationship, you overcame every challenge that stood in your way and grew as a person.

Why romance is important


Romance is an important part of our lifes, not just our culture, but also how we interact with and learn about the opposite sex (or the same sex if you're gay, I don't judge).

Kids learn at a very young age boys and girls are treated very differently by society, which dictates what each sex likes, wears, plays and works at. With the proper education, kids can learn to respect each other and get encouraged to pursue and discover a facet of themselves they might not have enjoyed otherwise. With the wrong education, you get the social issues we face nowadays.

The best romance stories I've read aren't about getting the girl and chasing a power fantasy. The best ones taught me valuable life lessons I still follow to this day, like respect, communication, trust and empathy. They're about characters meeting new people, liwing new experiences, observing their life with a different perspective, and by doing so learning more about themselves in the process; learning to listen to others' pleas and helping them solve their issues, not because there's something to gain by doing so, but simply because it's the right thing to do. That is the kind of story I want to see. That is a story worth telling, in my eyes.
